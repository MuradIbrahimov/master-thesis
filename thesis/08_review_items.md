## Interpretation issues requiring author or supervisor review

1. **Confirm final wording of the established H1–H5.** The matrix uses the author's working research-plan statements verbatim and maps saved evidence to each. Supervisor refinements should preserve the distinction between transition association (H2–H4) and predictive improvement (H5).
2. **Specify the evaluation claim.** Stage 05's 2020–26 period was the locked final test for the original model set. Stage 07 re-examined the same outcomes after introducing new indices, so that extension is exploratory. These statuses should remain distinct throughout the manuscript.
3. **GPR publication timing.** Archived point-in-time daily GPR vintages and release timestamps were not reconstructed. A previous-calendar-day reference date alone does not prove information availability. All GPR-based forecast findings must retain the phrase *historical pseudo-out-of-sample* unless this limitation is resolved by a separate future study.
4. **Return construction and irregular spacing.** Notebook 01 selects the immediately preceding **observed** oil price before checking whether both prices are positive. Its saved audit marks WTI 20 April 2020 (current price −36.98) and 21 April (previous observed price −36.98) undefined, and retains 22 April (+42.583%). In the primary 2000–15 training set, cumulative returns across missing-price gaps are treated as single observations in daily GARCH and Markov recursions. The 2007-start sensitivity changes both coverage quality and sample composition; it cannot identify the effect of gaps alone.
5. **M2 inference.** A high→low transition is approximately zero and has no standard error. Retain M2 for retrospective state description only; its favorable AIC/BIC is not sufficient for conventional inference on all transition coefficients.
6. **Near-integrated GARCH and boundary probabilities.** Both GARCH distributions have $\alpha+\beta$ near one and residual ARCH; some time-varying transition estimates approach 0 or 1. Estimation convergence does not remove these model limitations.
7. **Bootstrap convention and multiplicity.** Stage 05–06 files use centered absolute-tail p-values alongside basic equal-tail intervals. The WTI M1–M4 example demonstrates divergent 5% decisions; the appendix uses the equal-tail p ≈ 0.082 consistent with its interval (−0.005712, +0.067769). The original p = 0.0475 remains in Stage 06 as an audit trail, not as the thesis's primary inferential decision. Across many related comparisons, emphasize matched-date effect estimates, intervals, period consistency and the absence of multiplicity adjustment. Do not silently change saved Stage 05–06 tables.
8. **Substantive claim strength.** Main Brent GPR-entry and VIX-persistence contrasts are conditional associations; WTI and 2007-start intervals are weaker. M5's test gain over M1 and tiny gain over M4 do not have stable interval support. The negative result is central, not an inconvenience to be edited away.
9. **Calendar and unit language.** Expected regime durations are measured in *retained observations*. Return and SD values are percentage points; second moments and MSE inputs are squared percentage points. MSE *losses* are in fourth powers of return percentage points. The last test date is 26 October 2026, not a completed year 2026.
10. **Conservative VIX complete cases.** Notebook 01 constructs the VIX lag only on nonmissing same-day VIX rows and then requires both same-day and lagged VIX/GPR fields. The same-day VIX value is not an M3–M5 transition covariate. This excludes 85 valid Brent returns (51 training, 21 validation, 13 test); all have an earlier raw VIX quote. The original common sample is retained for comparability. Any alternative sample would require a separately justified lag construction and model refits, which are outside this drafting review.
