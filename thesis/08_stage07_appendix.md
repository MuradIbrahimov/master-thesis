# Appendix draft: retrospective AI and oil-specific geopolitical-risk indices

## Purpose and measurement

This extension asks whether a narrower news measure of geopolitical oil-supply disruption improves the historical Brent transition model beyond VIX. It **does not replace** the pre-existing Stage 05 forecast experiment. M4 contains lagged VIX only; M5 combines VIX with the original daily recent GPR series used in Stages 01–06. E1 substitutes the new general `GPR_AI` index for that original GPR; E2 substitutes `GPR_OIL`; E3 uses `GPR_AER`, the keyword-based comparator constructed on the **same three newspapers** as the AI indices. E3 is source-matched with E1 and E2, but it is not identical to the earlier recent GPR series drawn from broader newspaper coverage. All five models share a two-state Gaussian switching-mean and switching-variance observation equation. The extra predictor is standardized with estimation-window moments and is lagged to the previous calendar date. [Index definitions](../results/tables/07_index_definitions.csv), [scaling](../results/tables/07_scaling.csv), and [matched data](../data/processed/07_brent_ai_gpr_matched.csv) preserve the implementation.

The [AI-GPR source page](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/ai_gpr) and [Iacoviello and Tong (2026), *The AI-GPR Index: Measuring Geopolitical Risk using Artificial Intelligence*](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/research_files/AI_GPR_PAPER.pdf) describe a large-language-model assessment of geopolitical news across the *New York Times*, *Washington Post* and *Chicago Tribune*. The general AI measure scores geopolitical-risk intensity in articles; the oil measure adds an oil/energy-disruption classification layer to relevant geopolitical articles. The keyword comparator applies the earlier proximity method to these same sources. All are daily historical reconstructions, normalized under the source methodology, and use different concepts and distributions. A p10-to-p90 change in one index is therefore not the same-sized real-world shock as a p10-to-p90 change in another. The original source file and its SHA-256 are recorded in [source metadata](../results/tables/07_source_metadata.json).

Oil GPR is unusually sparse: **874 of 3,699 training observations (23.6%) are zero**. Its training p10 is zero and p90 is 241.67, versus p10 53.63 and p90 143.344 for general AI-GPR. The oil-index distribution suggests episodic coverage rather than a continuously graded risk measure at every Brent date. No additional observation is lost **relative to the established complete-case Brent sample** by adding the new indices: all five models use the identical 3,699 training, 999 validation and 1,712 later evaluation dates. That established sample already excludes 85 otherwise valid Brent returns under Notebook 01's conservative same-day-VIX rule; Stage 07 did not revisit that design. [Index coverage](../results/tables/07_data_coverage.csv), [fit attempts](../results/tables/07_fit_attempts.csv), [Notebook 01](../notebooks/01_data_preparation.ipynb), and [sample summary](../data/processed/01_sample_summary.csv) are the source.

## Fitted transition effects and model fit

The table reports training-model p90−p10 contrasts in percentage points, at the other predictor's training median, with conditional delta-method 95% intervals. Each contrast concerns the model's **own** fitted states, so it should not be interpreted as a controlled experiment that changes only the index definition.

| Model and GPR measure | Low→high contrast, points (95% interval) | High→high contrast, points (95% interval) |
|---|---:|---:|
| M5, original recent GPR | +3.566 (+1.066, +6.066) | −5.105 (−14.808, +4.598) |
| E1, general AI-GPR | +2.749 (+0.369, +5.130) | −4.388 (−12.286, +3.510) |
| E2, Oil GPR | −5.855 (−11.796, +0.086) | +0.381 (−14.405, +15.166) |
| E3, source-matched keyword GPR | +4.666 (+1.857, +7.475) | −10.379 (−20.454, −0.304) |

The Oil GPR E2 entry contrast has the opposite sign from M5, E1 and E3, and its interval narrowly includes zero. Its high-state persistence contrast is highly imprecise. The positive GPR entry association in the main Brent model thus does **not** generalize mechanically to an oil-specific news measure. E2's training AIC, 16,073.94, is 2.05 below M4's 16,075.99; BIC, 16,136.10, is 10.38 above M4's 16,125.72. Fit criteria therefore disagree even before held-out evaluation. [Scenario contrasts](../results/tables/07_transition_scenarios.csv), [model comparison](../results/tables/07_model_comparison.csv), and [contrast figure](../results/figures/07_transition_contrasts.png) support this paragraph.

All 30 recorded independent-start estimations converged without a captured warning or missing standard error, and likelihood spreads were small. However, E2's minimum training transition probability is about 0.000016, close enough to a boundary to limit easy asymptotic inference. Valid state probabilities and numerical convergence do not turn these conditional contrasts into causal effects. [Numerical stability](../results/tables/07_model_stability.csv) documents the checks.

## Historical forecast evaluation

The table reproduces the independent Stage 07 pipeline's matched-date losses, so its M4/M5 values differ in the last decimal places from Stage 05's separate fits. **The Stage 05 results remain the main forecast experiment.** Lower QLIKE, MSE and negative log score are preferred. The later period is called *exploratory* because 2020–26 outcomes were examined in earlier stages before the AI-index comparison.

| Model | Validation QLIKE | Validation MSE | Validation negative log score | Exploratory 2020–26 QLIKE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M4 VIX | 2.442050 | 89.642 | 2.130951 | 3.336389 |
| M5 original GPR + VIX | 2.445025 | 89.724 | 2.132303 | 3.335633 |
| E1 general AI-GPR + VIX | 2.438334 | 89.524 | 2.129998 | 3.344185 |
| E2 Oil GPR + VIX | 2.450443 | 89.886 | 2.133142 | 3.425109 |
| E3 matched keyword GPR + VIX | 2.436317 | 89.399 | 2.129821 | 3.369615 |

Oil GPR E2 is worse than M4 in validation QLIKE by 0.008393 and in the previously examined later period by 0.088720. The validation paired basic 95% interval for **M4−E2** is (−0.019953, +0.005073), with equal-tail p = 0.189; the interval includes zero, so a general Oil-GPR disadvantage is not firmly established on validation alone. The exploratory later interval is (−0.138068, −0.026307), but this period is not a fresh confirmatory test for Stage 07. E1 and E3 give small numerical validation gains over M4; their later ranking reverses and multiple index definitions were examined. Even E1's validation comparison with M5 (M5−E1 = +0.006691; interval +0.001569 to +0.011435) should be interpreted as exploratory under this multiplicity and historical reconstruction. The extension supplies no reliable claim that Oil GPR or a newer general index creates a deployable forecast advantage. [Validation performance](../results/tables/07_validation_forecast_comparison.csv), [exploratory performance](../results/tables/07_exploratory_test_comparison.csv), and [paired QLIKE comparisons](../results/tables/07_paired_qlike.csv) contain the exact outputs.

The AI indices were created in 2026 and backcast historically. Their observation-date values are **not verified archived real-time publications**. The same concern applies to revisions and release timing of the original daily GPR. The strict sequential filtering code prevents target-return leakage *conditional on those historical predictor values*, but cannot establish that a forecaster in 2016 or 2020 could have accessed the values used here. Moreover, comparing the new definitions on a period already examined in Stages 05–06 invites data-snooping. This is appropriately placed as a retrospective appendix and cannot overturn the principal held-out conclusion. [Source page](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/ai_gpr), [Stage 07 metadata](../results/tables/07_source_metadata.json), and [Stage 07 interpretation](../results/tables/07_interpretation.md) give the provenance and limits.

## Correction to the Stage 06 WTI bootstrap interpretation

The WTI test comparison of M1 minus M4 QLIKE has mean +0.036959, so its sign numerically favours M4. Stage 06 originally reported an absolute centered-bootstrap two-sided p-value 0.047476, while its **basic equal-tail 95% interval** was (−0.005712, +0.067769). For an asymmetric centered bootstrap distribution, the absolute-tail p and equal-tail interval need not agree on the 5% decision. A two-sided **equal-tail p-value** calculated from the same resamples is 0.081959, consistent with the interval containing zero. The manuscript uses approximately **p = 0.082** with that interval and does not declare a 5% WTI M4 advantage over M1. The arithmetic and forecasts were not changed; the original result remains an audit trail for the reporting-convention correction. Neither convention adjusts for multiple comparisons or nested-model nonregularity. [Correction table](../results/tables/07_stage06_bootstrap_correction.csv) and [correction note](../results/tables/07_stage06_bootstrap_correction.md) are the verified record.
