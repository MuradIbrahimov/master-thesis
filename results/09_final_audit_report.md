### A. Brent calendar and observation clock

The [raw/processed interval ledger](tables/09_calendar_interval_ledger.csv) and [annual coverage](tables/09_annual_calendar_coverage.csv) reproduce the 167 training intervals over three calendar days and maximum 11-day gap. The raw Brent file lists weekdays with blank prices: in 2000, 2001 and 2002 it has respectively 202, 194 and 194 positive price entries out of 260, 261 and 261 rows; the common-sample return counts are 197, 189 and 188. The common-case alignment removes only 5, 5 and 6 further returns in those years. Thus the sparse early sample is primarily a source-price coverage issue. The 276 training returns with more than one weekday of exposure pass at least one blank source row; the local file alone cannot establish whether a blank reflects a holiday closure or an unavailable quote. Keeping the pre-2000 source rows in the boundary audit avoids falsely classifying 31 December 1999 as an unlisted weekday.

The sensitivity retains **all** original training and validation dates. It divides each return by the square root of the number of intervening weekdays before fitting and rescales each one-step forecast mean, variance, second moment and density to the untransformed-return target. A normal Friday-to-Monday interval has exposure one. This tests a diffusion-style elapsed-weekday convention; it does **not** provide a continuous-time Markov likelihood or extra hidden transitions across gaps. Its holiday exposure assumption is imperfect. Deleting long returns would also alter the GARCH/Markov observation clock, so no such deletion is presented as a repaired primary sample. The existing 2007-start experiment remains a separate economic-period sensitivity.

### B. Same-day VIX exclusion

Notebook 01's lag construction first selects dates with observed *same-day* VIX, so its common sample excludes 85 otherwise usable Brent returns: 51 training, 21 validation and 13 test ([excluded dates](tables/09_same_day_vix_exclusions.csv)). That same-day value is absent from the Markov observation and transition equations. An as-of join to the latest **strictly earlier** observed VIX quote recovers all 85 dates without using a future VIX value; the original lag value is reproduced exactly on every retained date. The recovered quote is at most four calendar days old in training and three days old in validation/test ([recovered dates](tables/09_recovered_vix_lag_dates.csv)). Adding returns changes the observation-step state/filter path, so it is a robustness sample, not a silent correction to published estimates.

On the **original 999 validation dates**, the recovered-sample refits give QLIKE **M1 2.43924, M4 2.44199, M5 2.44410**. All 1,020 alternative dates give 2.43516, 2.43681 and 2.43881 respectively; those full-sample scores are not directly compared with original 999-date losses. M5's fitted GPR entry contrast remains positive at +3.35 points (+0.80 to +5.90), and VIX high-state persistence is +20.82 points (+3.72 to +37.92). The original qualitative forecast finding is unchanged ([performance](tables/09_vix_lag_only_validation_performance.csv), [scenarios](tables/09_sensitivity_transition_contrasts.csv)).

### C. GPR publication timing

The [official maintainer page](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/gpr.htm) states that recent daily GPR is updated on Mondays and that values can be revised. The linked [official archive](https://github.com/iacoviel/iacoviel.github.io/tree/master/gpr_archive_files), inspected through its [listing API](https://api.github.com/repos/iacoviel/iacoviel.github.io/contents/gpr_archive_files), starts with a named daily-recent vintage of **24 February 2022**. These filenames do not authenticate exact release times, and no 2000–2019 contemporaneous training/validation vintages were located. A genuine point-in-time GPR predictor cannot be reconstructed for the principal design from this evidence. See the [source assessment](tables/09_gpr_publication_timing.md).

As an **approximate** delay check, the same revised index is lagged seven calendar days, standardized on the training sample, and M5 is refitted on the original 3,699/999 dates. Its validation QLIKE is **2.44519**, versus unchanged M4 **2.44205** and original one-day-lag M5 **2.44502**. Its GPR entry contrast is +4.31 points (+1.43 to +7.20), and VIX persistence +23.44 points (+3.44 to +43.45). This supports a historical conditional association but no incremental validation forecast gain. The seven-day lag does **not** undo historical revisions or verify availability on any forecast date ([forecasts](tables/09_gpr_7day_proxy_validation_forecasts.csv), [fits/scenarios](tables/09_sensitivity_transition_contrasts.csv)). Therefore GPR-using forecasts remain revised-data **historical pseudo-out-of-sample** exercises, not verified real-time predictions.

### D–E. Incremental fit and transition uncertainty

M4 is nested in M5 by setting both GPR transition slopes to zero. Both have the same 3,699 return dates, Gaussian switching-mean/switching-variance observation equation and likelihood convention. Independent multistart refits reproduce log likelihoods **−8029.99711 (M4)** and **−8025.59062 (M5)**, with 8 and 10 parameters. The LR statistic is **8.81297**; a conventional χ²(2) reference gives nominal **p = 0.01220**. AIC prefers M5, while BIC prefers M4 ([LR table](tables/09_m4_m5_likelihood_ratio.csv), [original fit attempts](tables/09_original_markov_fit_attempts.csv), [Stage 04 comparison](tables/04_model_comparison.csv)). The nominal reference should not be the sole inferential basis with latent states and near-boundary transition probabilities.

A conditional parametric LR bootstrap simulated under fitted M4, held the VIX/GPR paths fixed, initialized the latent state at the first transition matrix's stationary distribution, and refitted M4/M5 from two starts per replication. **120/120** replications had valid selected fits; all 480 LR start-level fits converged without captured warnings. **0/120** simulated LR values exceeded 8.81297 (maximum 7.19586). The add-one tail estimate is **p = 0.00826**; with zero exceedances its resolution is limited and the exact one-sided 95% binomial upper bound for the simulated tail is about **0.0247**. This is conditional evidence of *in-sample* incremental GPR fit under a correctly specified Gaussian M4 null and observed exogenous paths. It does not account for possible model misspecification, correlated predictor generation, multiple prior comparisons or real-time GPR availability; it does not imply a forecast benefit ([replications](tables/09_parametric_bootstrap_replications.csv), [attempts](tables/09_parametric_bootstrap_fit_attempts.csv), [summary](tables/09_parametric_bootstrap_summary.csv)).

Independent analytic logistic calculations confirm variance-ordered regime labels, origin-to-destination orientation, training-only scaling, scenario percentiles and delta gradients/covariance indexing. Refit-to-saved discrepancies are at most **0.000161** in a point contrast and **0.000352** in a CI bound, with no interpretive effect ([verification](tables/09_original_transition_contrast_verification.csv)). The original M5 effects are about **+3.57 points** for GPR low-to-high and **+19.30 points** for VIX high-to-high. A separate **120/120** successful M5 parametric refit bootstrap gives percentile 95% intervals **+1.68 to +7.67 points** and **+10.67 to +34.91 points**, respectively. All 240 start-level effect fits converged without captured warnings. These model-based intervals corroborate the main Brent conditional contrasts, but they do not resolve mixed WTI, 2007-start or oil-specific-GPR evidence and are not multiplicity adjusted. No causal effect is identified.

### F. Forecast and paired-bootstrap verification

The independent check reconstructed all 14 Stage 05 model/period forecast series on identical dates. It verifies that each Markov forecast uses the previous **filtered** posterior and the current lagged transition matrix before assimilating the target return; no smoothed probabilities enter the forecast code. GARCH recursions likewise use the preceding observed return. The Gaussian mixture mean, second moment, variance and density, standardized Student-t density, QLIKE and negative log score reproduce within numerical tolerance (maximum saved-density discrepancy below **5×10⁻¹⁰**). Training-only and training-plus-validation scaling also reproduce ([checks](tables/09_forecast_consistency_checks.csv), [Stage 05 forecasts](tables/05_forecasts.csv)). This establishes no *target-return* leakage conditional on the saved predictors; it does not establish GPR release-time availability.

All saved Stage 05 paired block-bootstrap differences and basic intervals reproduce. A two-sided absolute centered-tail p-value need not agree with a basic **equal-tail** interval for an asymmetric bootstrap distribution. The WTI test M1−M4 QLIKE difference is **+0.036959**, the basic interval **[−0.005712, +0.067769]**, and the coherent equal-tail p-value **0.081959**; the original absolute-tail p **0.047476** remains in the Stage 06 audit trail. No 5% WTI M4 advantage should be claimed, especially after multiple model comparisons ([verification](tables/09_paired_bootstrap_verification.csv), [Stage 07 correction](tables/07_stage06_bootstrap_correction.csv)). None of the principal Brent paired loss intervals excludes zero. The previously examined 2020–2026 test period is used here only to verify saved arithmetic, not to choose the new sensitivities.

## Answers to the ten audit questions

1. **Implementation:** The principal return policy, two-state observation equations, transition orientation, forecast moments/densities and sequential updates are mathematically consistent with their stated observation-step models. M2 remains descriptive because of near-boundary transitions/missing standard errors; near-integrated GARCH is not a fully satisfactory variance model.
2. **Sample:** The historical sample is usable and documented, but early Brent blanks and the unnecessary same-day-VIX restriction require explicit sensitivity reporting.
3. **Transition robustness:** The fitted Brent GPR-entry and VIX-persistence directions survive the new gap, recovered-VIX and approximate delay checks. Earlier WTI, shortened-sample and oil-specific-index results remain mixed.
4. **Incremental GPR:** Conditional in-sample M4-versus-M5 LR evidence is detectable. There is **no convincing incremental validation forecasting evidence** beyond VIX, and the conditional LR is not a real-time or causal claim.
5. **Forecast leakage:** No target-return leakage or smoothed-state use was found in the saved Stage 05 sequential forecasts.
6. **Revised GPR:** Yes. Missing point-in-time vintages materially restrict all real-time forecasting language.
7. **Uncertainty:** Saved loss intervals reproduce; the WTI reporting convention is corrected. New conditional parametric intervals are coherent but depend on Gaussian-model correctness and are not multiplicity adjusted.
8. **Reproducibility:** Raw/processed relationships, saved forecasts, fits, seeds, versions and all seven preceding notebook execution records pass the specified checks. The exact historical GPR information set cannot be reproduced.
9. **Incorrect conclusions:** No major saved numerical estimate was shown to be wrong. The original WTI p/CI 5% interpretation was inconsistent and already corrected. H4 wording should now acknowledge the conditional in-sample LR evidence while preserving the negative predictive conclusion.
10. **Thesis standard:** The empirical analysis is methodologically viable for a KTU master's thesis **after** the identified wording and qualification changes are incorporated and reviewed. This is an econometric audit verdict, not institutional approval.
